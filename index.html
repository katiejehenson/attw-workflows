How Your Workflows
Work Together

A plain-English guide to the two-layer workflow system, what each workflow does, when to apply it, and what happens automatically vs. what you do manually.

The Big Picture

Your entire workflow system is built in two layers. Layer 1 — All Trips workflows fire for every single client, every time, no matter the destination. Layer 2 — Trip Type workflows layer on top for cruises, Disney, FIT trips, and more. You never repeat universal steps inside a trip-specific workflow — Layer 1 already handles them.

Layer 1 — Every Trip
  • 01 All Trips: Inbound Leads
  • 02 All Trips: Planning
  • 03 All Trips: Payments
  • 04 All Trips: Booked (Pre + Post)
Layer 2 — Trip Type (runs alongside)
  • 🚢 Mass Market Cruise
  • 🏰 Walt Disney World
  • 🚢 Disney Cruise Line
  • 🗺 FIT / Custom Itinerary
  • + any future trip types

Right now, most workflows are applied manually. The goal is auto-apply within the next few weeks. In the meantime: when a trip moves to Planning, manually apply the relevant Layer 2 workflow. When a trip is marked Booked, All Trips: Payments and All Trips: Booked will apply automatically.

Layer 1 — All Trips Workflows — One by One
01
All Trips: Inbound Leads
Trigger: Trip status → New Lead
Apply Manually ▾

Walks you through the first contact with a new client — from scheduling a discovery call through sending the call recap and getting the agency agreement signed. Email notifications for this workflow are turned off so they don't fire unexpectedly.

Tasks (you do these)
  • Schedule discovery call
  • Send call recap email (manual)
  • Send agency client agreement + planning fee
Automations
  • Email notifications off for this workflow — you control all sends
When does this fire? The trigger is trip status = New Lead — not trip creation. This means it only fires when you intentionally move a trip to New Lead, giving you full control over timing.
02
All Trips: Planning
Trigger: Trip status → Planning
Auto-Applies ✓ ▾

Fires as soon as the trip status changes to Planning. The assumption is that a trip moves to Planning because the client has signed the agreement and paid the planning fee.

Tasks (you do these)
  • Contact the DMC / preferred supplier
  • Create and send the proposal
  • Proposal follow-up if no response
  • Proposal updates as needed
Automations (fire on their own)
  • Immediately: Tern account setup email sent to client
  • 5 days later: Travel insurance introductory email
  • When client clicks "Approve Itinerary": Proposal approved email fires (only if they click it — not if you approve on their behalf)
Travel insurance email: This is a tap on the shoulder, not a hard sell. It includes a line in bold that says a preliminary quote will be included with the proposal. It is not delivering the quote itself — just setting the expectation that insurance will be discussed.
❓ Question for Christine: When do you send a client their traveler profile form — name, address, travel preferences, passport info? This does not need to be automated. It can be manually attached to the proposal email or sent separately. Let us know your preference so we can add it to the right touchpoint.
03
All Trips: Payments
Trigger: Trip status → Booked
Auto-Applies ✓ ▾

Fires as soon as a trip is marked Booked. Handles the entire payment lifecycle — from deposit through final payment. Because this is centralized here, you do not need payment reminder emails inside any trip-specific workflow.

Tasks (you do these)
  • Enter commission info with host agency
  • Mark trip as booked in Tern
  • Post-trip: verify commission has been received
Automations (fire on their own)
  • 10 days before payment due: Payment reminder email to client
  • 1 day before payment due: Final reminder email
  • When client authorizes payment: Acknowledgment email — "I'll go ahead and pay this for you"
  • Supplier payment task fires alongside the 10-day reminder
How Tern knows the payment dates: Payment due dates live inside the booking or package record in Tern. Make sure dates are entered in Tern when you set up the booking.
04
All Trips: Booked (Pre + Post Travel)
Trigger: Trip status → Booked · Runs alongside all trip-type workflows
Auto-Applies ✓ ▾

The universal pre- and post-travel workflow. Trip-type agnostic — works the same whether the client is on a cruise, a Disney trip, or a custom FIT. You never need to repeat these steps inside a trip-specific workflow.

Tasks (you do these)
  • 7 days after task creation: Add trip-specific tasks as needed — tickets, car rental, anything outside the standard list
  • 10 days after trip ends: Review trip feedback
  • 181 days after trip ends: Archive the trip
Automations — all Active ✓
  • 48-Hour Bon Voyage — 2 days before trip starts. Tern app link, itinerary link, online check-in reminder.
  • Welcome Home Survey — 2 days after trip ends. Feedback survey + photo request.
  • 6-Month Post-Travel Check-In — 180 days after trip ends. Re-engagement email, plants the seed for the next trip.
Arrival Day Check-In has moved. It now lives in the FIT Booked workflow since it's most relevant for land-based independent travel. Cruise clients get the Bon Voyage and Welcome Home emails from this workflow.
❓ Question for Christine: Do you regularly schedule pre-travel calls with clients? If so, we can add an automated email 30 days out asking them to book time with you for a trip review call. Let us know and we'll build it.
The Golden Rule — What You Never Need to Repeat
  1. Commission tracking is in All Trips: Payments. Don't add a GRASP task to your Disney or FIT workflow.
  2. Payment reminders (10-day, 1-day, authorization) are in All Trips: Payments. Trip-specific workflows don't need their own.
  3. Bon Voyage, welcome home survey, and 6-month follow-up are in All Trips: Booked. The Arrival Day Check-In lives in FIT Booked only.
  4. Pre-travel document wrap-up (22 days out) is in All Trips: Booked. Your Disney and cruise workflows don't need their own version.

Disney Cruise Line
For: Disney Wish, Fantasy, Dream, Wonder, Magic
Apply at Planning ▾

Covers what's unique to DCL — port adventure booking windows, adult dining, Navigator app setup, pre-cruise hotel stays, and online check-in. Every automation in the Core Planning workflow is currently Inactive with no trigger date set. Work through the priority list below.

💡 Tern Library tip: When you add a Disney Cruise from the Tern library, ship details including deck plans, dining, recreation, and kids clubs are added automatically. For adult dining specifics, the recommendation is to create Info Blocks in Tern — one per ship (e.g., "Adult Dining — Disney Wish"). Your DCL email then points clients to their ship's info block. This keeps everything in Tern, reinforces it as their one-stop resource, and eliminates the need for ship-specific email variations the platform can't currently trigger automatically.
